# Twin Tech-sudo sales starter kit

This kit supports the first public offer: the **Workflow Clarity Review** for UK construction and document-heavy teams. It is deliberately usable before incorporation, trade mark filing, analytics or the custom domain are complete. Do not describe Twin Tech-sudo as a limited company and do not use `®`.

## First customer profile

Prioritise businesses where staff repeatedly read, copy, compare, check or re-key construction documents. The initial interview list can include estimators, quantity surveyors, commercial managers, pre-construction teams, main contractors and specialist subcontractors.

A strong first conversation has all four:

- a named person who owns or performs the workflow;
- a repeated document journey with a clear beginning and output;
- visible handling time, delay, rework or error risk;
- safe access to representative, redacted material.

Do not force a software sale where process changes or an existing product are the better answer.

## Short positioning statement

Twin Tech-sudo helps UK construction and document-heavy teams improve repeated workflows. We map the current process, assess where automation is realistic and design review-first software only when the evidence supports a build.

## Introductory outreach

Subject: A quick question about your document workflow

> Hi [name], I am researching how [estimating/commercial/pre-construction] teams handle repeated document work such as [specific task]. I am not looking for confidential files or trying to sell a large software build. I would value 20 minutes to understand what happens today, where the friction sits and what a genuinely useful improvement would look like. Would you be open to a short conversation next week?

Follow-up after five to seven working days:

> Hi [name], just bringing this back to the top of your inbox. I am speaking with construction teams about repeated document workflows and would value your practical perspective. A short “not a priority” reply is useful too.

## 20-minute interview guide

Ask about real events, not hypothetical feature preferences.

1. What repeated document task did you last complete?
2. What triggered it, and what output was needed?
3. Which documents, spreadsheets, emails or systems were involved?
4. Who touched the work, and where did it wait?
5. Which details required judgement rather than copying?
6. What exceptions or mistakes did you have to catch?
7. How did you check the result and retain evidence?
8. What happens when the input format changes?
9. Roughly how often does this occur and how long does a typical example take?
10. What would need to remain under human control?
11. What have you already tried?
12. If one part improved, which outcome would matter most?

Close with: “May I summarise what I heard and come back with the smallest sensible next step?” Do not promise automation, savings or accuracy during the interview.

## Workflow Clarity Review outline

Agree the exact scope, schedule, price, confidentiality terms and ownership in writing before starting. The public site intentionally does not publish a price until delivery effort has been tested.

### Inputs

- one named workflow and owner;
- one or two working sessions;
- representative redacted documents or screenshots;
- current steps, systems, hand-offs and known exceptions;
- any available baseline for time, volume, rework or delay.

### Outputs

- current-workflow map;
- friction, risk and evidence notes;
- one bounded first-use-case definition;
- baseline and proposed success measure;
- recommendation: stop, process change, off-the-shelf product, prototype, pilot or custom build.

### Boundaries

- no production software is included unless separately agreed;
- no accuracy, saving or return-on-investment claim is made before testing;
- confidential files are not sent through ordinary email;
- AI output does not replace professional construction or commercial judgement.

## Case study capture

For every project, capture:

1. the original workflow and constraint;
2. the agreed scope and what was excluded;
3. screenshots or diagrams the customer permits you to show;
4. the baseline, method and measurement window;
5. the result, including limitations and remaining human review;
6. written permission for the exact public wording.

Until evidence is available, describe the problem, product direction and delivered scope without implying a measured commercial result.

## Four-week owner routine

### Week 1

- List 20 relevant people or businesses already within reach.
- Send five tailored interview requests.
- Run at least two interviews and complete the qualification notes.

### Week 2

- Run three more interviews.
- Group repeated pains by workflow, role and document type.
- Revise the offer language only if the interviews contradict the current focus.

### Week 3

- Choose one representative workflow.
- Produce a redacted workflow diagram or prototype using safe sample material.
- Write a short, factual demonstration page without invented results.

### Week 4

- Offer the Workflow Clarity Review to the strongest qualified contacts.
- Agree a small, explicit scope with one prospect.
- Start the evidence log before delivery begins.
